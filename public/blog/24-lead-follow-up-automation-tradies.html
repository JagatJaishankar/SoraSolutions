You just quoted a $15,000 bathroom renovation. The homeowner nodded, said "looks great, I'll think about it," and walked you out. You had three other jobs on the go, so you told yourself you'd follow up later. You never did. Three weeks later, the same homeowner posted in a local Facebook group — a glowing review for a competitor who gave them a call the next day.

Sound familiar? This is the single most expensive habit in the trades. Not bad marketing. Not a broken website. Not even pricing. It's the leads that were already warm — enquiries, quotes, website forms — that simply never heard back from you.

The good news: this is entirely fixable with automation. A well-built follow-up system works while you're on the tools, asleep, or on a Sunday arvo with the family. Here's exactly how it works.

The Follow-Up Problem Every Tradie Has

Ask any tradie if they follow up on every lead and most will say "yeah, usually." Ask them how many times and the answer is almost always once — or zero. It's not laziness. It's the reality of running a trade business: you're flat out on site, phone calls happen between jobs, and by the time you've finished a long day you've completely forgotten about the bathroom reno guy from last Thursday.

The result? Leads leak out of your business like water through a cracked pipe. A potential customer fills in your contact form at 9pm, you see it the next morning at 7am on site, you think "I'll call at lunch," and by 3pm it's buried under twelve other notifications. They've already booked someone else.

Here's what makes this worse: people enquire with multiple tradies at once. When someone needs a plumber for a burst pipe or an electrician for a switchboard upgrade, they're not waiting around. They're calling three or four businesses in the first ten minutes. The one who responds first — even with just an automated text — wins the conversation.

!

Common mistake: Most tradies treat follow-up as a courtesy call. It's not — it's a sales activity that directly determines whether a warm lead books with you or your competitor. Treat every unreplied quote as money sitting on the table.

The pattern is almost always the same: Enquiry comes in → tradie responds when convenient → one follow-up if they remember → lead goes cold → tradie moves on. Meanwhile, the lead who didn't book is still out there needing the work done — they just found someone else who was more persistent.

What an Automated Follow-Up Looks Like

A properly built automated follow-up sequence doesn't feel like a robot. When written in plain, conversational language and timed correctly, it reads like a text from the business owner checking in. Here's exactly what a 7-day, 5-message sequence looks like for a tradie using a CRM like GoHighLevel.

The critical detail: the sequence stops the moment someone replies or books. It's not a spam blast — it's a smart sequence that knows when to step aside. If Sarah texts back after Day 1 saying "yes let's go ahead," the automation stops and you get a notification to jump in personally.

✍

Writing tip: Always use the customer's first name in every message. Mention the specific service they enquired about. Include your own name at the end. These three things alone make automated messages feel personal and human — not bulk SMS blasts.

Frequently Asked Questions

How quickly should I follow up on a new tradie lead?

Within 5 minutes if at all possible. Research shows that responding within 5 minutes makes you 21 times more likely to convert that lead versus waiting 30 minutes or more. An automated system replies instantly — even at 11pm on a Sunday when you're off the tools.

How many follow-ups should I send before giving up on a lead?

At least five. Studies show 80% of sales happen between the 5th and 12th contact, yet most tradies give up after one try. A 7-day automated sequence covering Day 0 (instant), Day 1, Day 3, Day 5, and Day 7 will capture the majority of converts without feeling pushy.

What platform should I use to automate lead follow-up?

GoHighLevel is the platform Sora deploys for every client. It handles instant lead capture from your website and ads, fires automated SMS and email sequences, tracks every touchpoint, and notifies you when a lead is ready to book — all without manual intervention.

Will automated follow-up messages feel robotic to my customers?

Not when written properly. The key is personalisation tokens (customer name, service requested, suburb) and a conversational tone. A well-crafted automated SMS feels like a quick text from the business owner. Sora writes every sequence in plain Australian English — not marketing-speak.
